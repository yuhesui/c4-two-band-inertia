# Manuscript abstract

We study $(s+1)$-regular $C_4$-free graphs of order $s^2+s+4$, a near-extremal regime in which local codegree counting produces a cubic zero-codegree child $D=M\mathbin{\dot\cup}F$ and, on the nonprincipal subspace, the relation $A^2=sI-D$. Thus the nonprincipal spectrum of $A$ is confined to two narrow bands around $\pm\sqrt{s}$. To exploit this concentration uniformly, we prove an all-orders polynomial theorem that recovers the signature of a Hermitian matrix from finitely many odd moments, with an explicit incomplete-beta error bound. The degree-five case yields a square-defect localization: for all sufficiently large even $s$, a graph in our family can exist only when $s=(4q+2)^2-6$ or $(4q+2)^2-8$. Consequently, a one-vertex improvement for $R(C_4,K_{1,s^2+3})$ holds for a density-one set of even parameters. We then use the next odd moment and the commutation of $A$ and $D$ to obtain a structural dichotomy for the $2$-factor $F$: every factor cycle is active or inactive, and an inactive factor edge induces a perfect matching between its two graph neighborhoods. The two surviving square-defect families have sharply different asymptotic active-cycle geometries, with active density tending to $0$ and $2/3$, respectively.
